Velum.

Holder-attested compliance for Confidential Tokens on Stellar.

A regulated fund can now hide its register of holders and prove it obeys the rules — identity-gated confidential tokens, plus on-chain proof that a position clears a regulatory threshold, without revealing the amount to anyone. Including the verifier.

Compliance has two halves. Upstream ships the primitives for both, wired to neither.

The questionUpstream todayVelum
Who may hold? A flat address allowlist. No reason, no expiry, no attesting party — insufficient for a securities regime. Bridging to the RWA identity registry is their issue #766, still open. velum-policy — the token asks an ERC-3643/T-REX identity registry: does this holder carry a valid qualified-investor claim from an approved issuer?
How much may they hold? Structurally impossible. is_authorized(account, token) -> bool sees no value — balances are Pedersen commitments. The RWA modules that enforce caps demand cleartext amount: i128. The two cannot meet. disclose_balance_ge + velum-attest — the holder proves "my position ≥ T" and a Soroban contract verifies it. Nothing is decrypted; there is nothing to decrypt.

Why the check had to move

Under FHE a contract compares ciphertexts with no witness, so the check fits inside the transfer. Under Pedersen commitments a proof needs a witness — and for any balance exactly one party holds it. A sender cannot prove anything about the recipient's balance: it has no opening for it, and binding guarantees it cannot find another.

Thesis. Under commitment-based confidentiality, quantitative compliance cannot be transaction-gated. It must be holder-attested — the party that can prove is the party whose balance it is.

Not a weaker guarantee: a differently-shaped one. For thresholds that qualify a holder — the qualified-investor minimum, a concentration ceiling — it is exactly what the regulation asks.

The problem, in their words

COMPLIANCE.md:218 — on why individual clawback is unsolved:

"Because the clawback circuit does not have access to vk_A… The follow-up revision will pin down whether r_s is supplied as a private witness with an auxiliary opening proof or derived in-circuit from a separately escrowed value."

They name the obstacle and list the remedy. We take that second option and say what to escrow: vk_A itself. Verified in code and on-chain: premise, circuit, and a seizure proof accepted by a Soroban verifier.

Every claim we make about their product is sourced to a file and line in Appendix A of the whitepaper — trait signatures and verbatim quotes, not our characterisation.

Anchor case

A Brazilian receivables fund (FIDC) under CVM 175, tokenized in senior and subordinated classes — Plina Finance. Today its register is public: any competitor reads who subscribed, how much, and when they left. The kit serves any regulated issuer with the same paradox.
