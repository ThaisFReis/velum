VELUM — Holder-attested compliance for Confidential Tokens on Stellar
Sub-lane: Enterprise, Compliance and RWA (OpenZeppelin + Nethermind)
Repository: https://github.com/ThaisFReis/velum  ·  MIT  ·  live on testnet


WHAT IT IS

A compliance kit that lets a regulated fund keep its register of holders private
and still prove it obeys the rules — without revealing any amount to anyone,
including the verifier.


THE PROBLEM

Compliance asks two questions of a fund's register, and OpenZeppelin's
stellar-contracts ships the primitives for both, wired to neither.

Who may hold? Upstream's confidential token gates on a flat address allowlist —
no reason, no expiry, no attesting party. Bridging it to the RWA identity
registry is their own issue #766, open since July.

How much may they hold? This one is structurally impossible at that interface.
`is_authorized(account, token) -> bool` receives an address and never a value,
because balances are Pedersen commitments. The RWA modules that enforce caps
demand a cleartext amount. The two cannot meet.

Under FHE a contract compares ciphertexts with no witness, so the check fits
inside the transfer. Under commitments a proof needs a witness, and for any
balance exactly one party holds it. So the check has to move: quantitative
compliance cannot be transaction-gated, it must be holder-attested.


WHAT WE BUILT

- velum-policy — an adapter from the token's authorization gate to an
  ERC-3643/T-REX identity registry: does this holder carry a valid claim from an
  approved issuer? It also fails closed on a registry misconfiguration that
  upstream ignores silently.
- disclose_balance_ge + velum-attest — the predicate circuit upstream specifies
  and does not ship, plus a Soroban contract that verifies it on-chain. The
  holder proves "my position >= T"; nothing is decrypted, because there is
  nothing to decrypt.
- circuits/seize + velum-seize — an answer to upstream's open question on
  individual clawback. A partial seizure, proven bounded by a position the
  ledger never reveals, and verified on-chain.
- profiles/cvm175.json — a jurisdiction as configuration, annotated with what is
  actually enforced and what is merely declared.

Everything runs on an unmodified upstream token. Nothing in stellar-contracts or
in the reference demo was patched to make it work. Study clones live in a
git-ignored folder, so no one else's code is in this repository.

Three demo scripts and an adversarial suite run against these contracts. The
suite probes what the system should refuse — one holder's proof submitted for
another account, a proof replayed after the balance moved, a stranger
submitting a valid seizure, an amount changed after proving. Nine probes, all
refused as designed.


WHAT WE FOUND IN UPSTREAM

Seven defects, each with reproduction. Six are documentation, tooling or
behaviour. The seventh is a soundness gap in the specification itself: its
disclosure flow resolves only the spendable commitment, which is harmless for a
minimum-holding rule and evadable for the concentration ceiling the same
sentence names — park value on the receiving side and you are under any cap. We
bind both commitments.


WHAT WE ARE NOT CLAIMING

Not production: developer-preview cryptography, the verifier is unaudited,
testnet only. Not finished: the seizure verifies but no value moves, and the key
escrow behind it is designed and priced, not built. Not solved: predicates over
encrypted aggregates across accounts remain open. Not invented: we implement
their specification, and the novelty claim is bounded to what we can verify —
no public implementation exists in any repository, branch, tag or history.
